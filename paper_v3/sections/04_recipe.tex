\section{From profile to recipe: components matter, ratios do not}
\label{sec:recipe}

The natural expectation --- and our preregistered one --- was that rescue
tracks the match between component and bucket. It does not
(Figure~\ref{fig:recipe}a). Measured against the placebo delta, only the
format component shows a large specific own-bucket effect ($+70$--$79$pp in
mixtures); conduct, phrasing, scaffold and rule sit within $\pm$16pp of the
placebo, whose own rescue floor is high (73\% of the conduct bucket).
Preregistered coefficients of .95 (conduct) and .90 (rule) were misses; the
replacement picture is a high non-specific floor with sparse specific signal
(Appendix~\ref{app:scorecard}).

Mixture ratios follow the same logic (Figure~\ref{fig:recipe}b). The
profile-matched mixture (A1), the uniform mixture (B), and the deliberately
reversed mixture (D) are indistinguishable on plain-genre rescue, and in the
repair genre matched ratios never exceeded uniform in any reading (B
$.717\pm.040$ vs A1 $.676\pm.025$ over three seeds). What does matter is
presence and toxicity: dropping the format component costs the entire format
rescue, and the drills component --- bare ``compute $X$'' items included as a
falsification arm --- is actively harmful, teaching the model to adopt planted
values ($W_{\text{adopt}}$ 25\% at 100\% dose) and sitting below the placebo
on the conduct bucket.

Dose curves sharpen both statements (Figure~\ref{fig:dose}). Format rescue
saturates at a 10\% dose --- sixty items --- and the toxicity of the pure diet
does not creep in with dose: every mixed dose passes the health gates, and
only the 100\% diet is lethal. Drills harm is likewise not monotone in dose on
plain items, but it is visible at a 25\% dose in the repair genre
(\S\ref{sec:genre}). The actionable output of diagnosis is therefore a
\emph{shopping list}, not a mixing ratio: add the specific components (cheaply),
exclude the toxic ones, and let any clean data supply the floor.
