\section{The diagnostic profile: what a benchmark score hides}
\label{sec:profile}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig1_profile.pdf}
\caption{A benchmark score hides a failure profile. Left: of GSM8K's 93.5
surface score, only 60.6\% of items survive interface perturbation; the failure
mass decomposes into credulity (conduct, 14.4\%), format coupling (14.7\%),
phrasing sensitivity, assistance-recoverable buckets, multi-signature items (3.7\%), and
unresolved (1.9\%). Right: the GSM-hard bucket is dominated by unresolved
failures (86.3\%). Including multi-signature memberships, conduct totals
16.4\% and phrasing 6.4\% (reconciliation footnote). Denominators, pool
filters, and the labile-core discount are given in Appendix~\ref{app:instruments}.}
\label{fig:profile}
\end{figure}

What does a 93.5 actually certify? We ran the eight-probe family over the
full test pool to find out, and the answer is: much less than the number
suggests. Figure~\ref{fig:profile} is the result. The headline is a gap: the model scores
93.5 on GSM8K, but only 60.6\% of items are answered correctly \emph{and}
survive every interface perturbation. The largest failure class is not
inability but \emph{credulity}: 14.4\% of items carry a pure conduct signature
(16.4\% including multi-signature items; segment heights in
Figure~\ref{fig:profile} are exclusive --- reconciliation in the caption) are solved correctly in
isolation yet flipped by a planted wrong intermediate or wrong final claim.
Within that class the failure is overwhelmingly \emph{derailment} rather than
\emph{adoption} (227 vs 9 items): the planted value knocks the computation off
course, but the model almost never copies it verbatim. Format coupling is the
second class (14.7\%): the same computation requested under a JSON constraint
fails, consistent with prior reports that format constraints degrade reasoning,
here established by item-level controlled pairing.

Two properties of the profile matter for everything downstream. First, the
buckets are not equally ``real'' as repair demand: a flip-overlap audit
(Appendix, Figure~\ref{fig:overlap}) shows that 57\% of the conduct bucket is
rescued by \emph{any} fine-tuning, including a clean-replay placebo --- a
deterministic labile core --- while the format bucket has essentially no free
core (1\%). Second, the profile is pool-dependent: on GSM-hard, 86.3\% of
items fail every probe, so the taxonomy above describes the regime where the
model is basically competent and fails at interfaces, not the regime where it
cannot solve the task at all.
